How a driver opts in

  1. At the warehouse kiosk, the driver selects English or Español and opens Driver Check-In.
  2. The driver enters their check-in details and mobile phone number. Entering a number alone does not grant SMS consent.
  3. A separate, initially unchecked SMS consent checkbox appears below the phone field. The disclosure and adjacent policy links are reproduced below.
  4. The driver may actively select the checkbox, then submit the check-in. Consent is optional; the driver can complete check-in with the checkbox left unchecked and obtain updates from warehouse staff.

This verified workflow uses kiosk checkbox consent. No verbal, purchased-list, or keyword enrollment pathway is represented by this evidence.

English disclosure shown in the kiosk

I agree to receive text messages from BluePulse Logistics LLC about my dock appointment status (check-in confirmation, dock assignment, load ready). Message frequency varies per visit. Message & data rates may apply. Reply STOP to opt out, HELP for help. Consent is optional and is not required to check in or purchase services. Ask warehouse staff for updates if you decline.

Verification and scope

  • The live staging page was checked in English and Spanish. The checkbox is unchecked by default, is not required, and returns to unchecked when the form is cancelled and reopened.
  • Source review and isolated tests verified that only the explicit checkbox value creates affirmative consent. The affirmative record includes a timestamp, language, and disclosure text; absent consent is recorded as no consent.
  • The verified door-assignment SMS path checks the stored consent before sending. Door assignment can still proceed without sending a driver SMS when consent is absent.
  • A later affirmative submission can add consent to the same visit. An unchecked duplicate submission does not revoke an earlier opt-in; STOP is the advertised SMS opt-out method.
  • Tests mocked database and provider operations. No test driver records or SMS were created, no queued messages were released, and production was not changed. Carrier delivery and STOP/HELP responses were not exercised by these tests.

The disclosure describes the intended operational notification program. Door assignment was the sending path examined for this verification; this document does not claim every notification type was tested.
